    Overview of Piles (Hemorrhoids)

    Piles, also known as hemorrhoids, are swollen veins in the lower rectum or around the anus. They are a common condition that can cause discomfort, bleeding, and irritation.

    What Are Piles?

    Piles are enlarged and inflamed blood vessels that develop inside the rectum (internal piles) or under the skin around the anus (external piles). They occur due to increased pressure during bowel movements, chronic constipation, pregnancy, or prolonged sitting.

    How to Identify Piles?

    • Bright red bleeding during or after passing stool
    • Pain or discomfort while sitting
    • Swelling or lump near the anus
    • Persistent itching or irritation
    • Feeling of incomplete bowel movement

    Common Symptoms

    • Painless bleeding (especially in internal piles)
    • Painful swelling around the anus
    • Mucus discharge
    • Anal itching and burning sensation
    • Lump that may protrude during straining

    Piles Treatment Options

    Piles can be treated using modern laser technology or conventional surgical methods. The right treatment depends on the severity and grade of hemorrhoids.

    Laser Piles Treatment

    • Minimally invasive procedure
    • Less pain and minimal bleeding
    • Faster recovery time
    • Short hospital stay (same-day discharge)
    • Lower risk of complications

    Conventional Surgery

    • Traditional hemorrhoid removal
    • May involve stitches
    • Longer recovery period
    • Possible post-surgery discomfort
    • Recommended for advanced cases

    Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

    Is piles treatment painful?

    Modern laser piles treatment is minimally invasive and causes very little pain. Most patients recover quickly with minimal discomfort.

    How long does piles surgery take?

    Laser piles surgery usually takes 20–30 minutes and is often done as a same-day procedure.

    Can piles go away without surgery?

    Mild piles may improve with medication, diet changes, and lifestyle modifications. However, advanced grades may require surgical treatment.

    When should I see a doctor for piles?

    You should consult a doctor if you experience persistent bleeding, severe pain, or swelling that does not improve within a few days.
