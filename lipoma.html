    Overview of Lipoma

    A lipoma is a soft, slow-growing, non-cancerous lump made up of fat tissue. It usually develops just under the skin and is commonly found on the neck, shoulders, back, arms, or thighs.

    What Is a Lipoma?

    A lipoma is a benign (non-cancerous) growth of fatty tissue that forms between the skin and underlying muscle layer. It feels soft to touch, moves easily when pressed, and usually grows slowly over time. Lipomas are generally painless but may cause discomfort if they press on nearby nerves.

    How to Identify a Lipoma?

    • Soft, doughy lump under the skin
    • Moves slightly when pressed
    • Usually painless
    • Slow-growing over months or years
    • Commonly found on neck, shoulders, back, or arms

    Common Symptoms & Concerns

    • Visible lump under the skin
    • Mild discomfort if pressing on nerves
    • Cosmetic concerns due to size
    • Rarely painful if inflamed
    • Increase in size over time

    Lipoma Treatment Options

    Lipoma treatment depends on the size, location, and symptoms of the lump. While small and painless lipomas may not require immediate removal, surgical procedures are recommended if the lipoma causes discomfort, pain, or cosmetic concerns.

    Laser Lipoma Removal

    • Minimally invasive and advanced technique
    • Smaller incision with minimal bleeding
    • Precise removal of fatty tissue
    • Reduced scarring compared to traditional surgery
    • Faster recovery and same-day discharge in most cases

    Conventional Lipoma Surgery

    • Traditional surgical excision using scalpel
    • May require stitches depending on size
    • Effective for large or deep lipomas
    • Slightly longer healing time
    • Recommended for complex or multiple lipomas

    Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

    What is a lipoma?

    A lipoma is a soft, slow-growing lump made of fatty tissue that develops under the skin. It is usually painless, movable, and non-cancerous. Lipomas commonly appear on the neck, shoulders, back, arms, or thighs.

    Is lipoma removal painful?

    Lipoma removal is typically performed under local anesthesia, making the procedure painless. Patients may experience mild discomfort after surgery, which can be managed with medications.

    When should a lipoma be removed?

    Removal is recommended if the lipoma causes pain, increases in size, restricts movement, creates cosmetic concerns, or shows unusual changes that require medical evaluation.

    How long does lipoma surgery take?

    Lipoma removal usually takes 20–40 minutes, depending on the size and location of the lump. Most procedures are done as same-day surgery.

    Can a lipoma come back after removal?

    Recurrence is rare when the lipoma is completely removed. However, in some cases, new lipomas may develop in other areas of the body.
